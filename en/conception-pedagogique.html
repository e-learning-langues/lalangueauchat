Instructional design

Sometimes GenAI speeds up the work; sometimes it makes possible what was not possible before; sometimes it even wastes our time. Ultimately, it does not necessarily let us work less: it lets us go further in creativity, multimodality and pedagogical ambition.

Nothing GenAI produces should be used without being reread, corrected and reworked by the teacher.

1. Engage and immerse

Learning objective: Spark students’ interest by immersing them in a story.
Added value: GenAI’s creativity makes it possible to write scenarios that a teacher in charge of course design could not necessarily create in a limited time.

Storytelling that continues from week to week, or within a module

GenAI writes the scenario that links all the units together, following a logical progression, in a given narrative style: week after week, students move forward through the story until the ending.

Show the exampleHide the example

For example, in WordIA, the themes and content of three “classic” units preparing for the B1 English CLES (French university language certification), originally independent of one another, were submitted to a chatbot. The result: the student plays an alien discovering 21st-century society (the CLES articles deal with current affairs). The alien has to complete three missions before returning to their planet, each corresponding to one unit. They begin by meeting Sam, a human student who tells them about tuition fees in the United States (unit on American campuses), then discuss the sharing economy with him (unit on loneliness) and debate the role of government in supporting the costs of parenthood and education (unit on the decision whether or not to have children). In the end, the alien falls in love with an Earthling: will they stay on Earth or go back to their planet?

Watch the WordIA scenario (video)

Branching scenarios

GenAI helps create a scenario based on the teacher’s sources in which students make decisions that lead them to different pages, while ensuring that all students will have covered the same concepts. GenAI can suggest the situations and the wording, but the teacher controls the progression and validates the pedagogical consequences of the choices.

Show the exampleHide the example

For example, in Lingua Franca, A2 students have to choose the character’s next reaction during a series of critical incidents. There is no right or wrong answer, but every decision has a consequence. The aim is to introduce interculturality while placing students in real-life situations through game mechanics, as a way into the topic before moving on to the theoretical concepts.

Watch the Lingua Franca scenario (video)

Choose-your-own-adventure game

We write a detailed prompt asking GenAI to create a choose-your-own-adventure game on the theme of the unit, in which the grammar and/or vocabulary of the course are either introduced or recycled. GenAI creates new situations as the student makes choices.

Show the exampleHide the example

For example, in Core, students have to devise a public health reform to improve equity and the health of city dwellers. They must choose whether to focus on primary care, universal health coverage or integrated care models. Each answer ticked generates a new set of options; each option has consequences. The course words listed in the prompt are woven logically into the story, so that students encounter them in context.

Watch the game on video

Playful and humorous immersion

Language skills are part of the narrative: the teacher gives GenAI details of the language points to be mastered, asking for several suggestions of comic scenarios and a game-based learning approach. GenAI suggests; the teacher evaluates, selects and reworks.

New concepts are introduced and illustrated by GenAI as the characters, who remain the same from start to finish, develop. All the example sentences, voiced with GenAI, relate to the story.

Show the exampleHide the example

For example, in a course on punctuation, the student is Professor Higgins’s research assistant. His article is finally ready for publication. But nobody had reckoned with the cat, Lord Byron, who took a nap on the keyboard, deleting or moving the punctuation marks. If the student sends the manuscript as it is, it will be rejected and the professor’s career will be over. The student has 20 minutes to fix everything before Higgins comes back. It’s a CAT-ASTROPHE.

In addition, in a module on silent letters in English, we worked with GenAI to develop the idea of a scenario in which students are supported by a voice coach to improve their pronunciation so they can audition for an English-language film called The Silent Quest, produced by Sound Stage Studios.

Finally, in a course on linking nouns, the student has to help their sister look after three children during a nappy shortage. Each room in the flat is associated with a problem to solve (the broken bottle warmer, the lost socks, the screaming twins, nap time…), right up to the final resolution.

Open the activity on nouns (Genially)

Course trailers

The trailer script is written from the teacher’s notes, corrected and then recorded with GenAI. Editing is done by hand, but some images and videos can be generated by GenAI if no equivalent exists.

2. Contextualise

Learning objective: Contextualise isolated content.
Added value: We give GenAI the “pen” and/or the “voice” we lack: it comes up with varied texts linked to the theme of the unit, which a teacher in charge of course design would not be able to write in a limited time.

Fictional specialist articles to discover vocabulary in context

To provide a first exposure in context to new specialist terms that will reappear in the unit, the teacher gives GenAI a list and asks for topic suggestions so that these words appear logically in a given context. Once the theme has been chosen, the teacher has GenAI generate an article and trains it on the genre and style, while correcting errors.

Show the exampleHide the example

For example, in an English course for public health, articles on professional ethics, well-being in hospitals and inter-team collaboration were produced by GenAI, which had to introduce, logically, a list of specialist words whose meaning can be inferred from context, for B1 level. The series of 8 articles is called “An ideal world” and students are told that the facts are invented.

Read a fictional article (PDF)

Contextualised exercises

In comprehension and language exercises, the examples and situations are built around the theme of the unit in order to recycle the vocabulary. Students no longer work only on isolated sentences, but also on paragraphs, texts and a variety of genres, so that they connect what they have learnt to situations close to real life.

Show the exampleHide the example

For example, in a module on expressing purpose, students are asked to complete a gap-fill text in the form of an email rather than isolated sentences. In a module on adjectives, they complete a police report. In a module on fillers, students are invited to complete a dialogue between two characters they have already met during the lesson.

View exercises (PDF)

Avatar news reports that put grammar in context

Audio and video reports are created to link the week’s grammar points to a context related to the students’ field of study, showing that these concepts will be useful in real life. For example, in the public health English course Expansion, an avatar announces new advances in public health. The prompt used to generate the script required several forms of the conditional as well as sentences in reported speech, the two grammar points of that period. Students listen to and observe the sentences, work out the rules, then move on to the lessons and practice.

Watch an avatar news report (video)

Songs whose lyrics illustrate a language feature or help learners remember it

Song lyrics are generated with one GenAI tool, and the complete tracks with another, to illustrate a language feature being studied. For example, in the Life Hacks A2+ course, songs illustrate how English words are pronounced depending on their part of speech and their number of syllables.

3. Offer linguistic variety

Learning objective: Expose students to new accents and voices.
Added value: The Espace Langues cannot call on speakers of every nationality.

Weekly introductory videos with a variety of accents to guess

Each week, an avatar introduces the objectives of the unit… with a different accent! The video has a twofold aim: to have students hear and identify an English-speaking accent as part of the course on intercultural communication, and to inform them of what is expected.

Watch an introductory video

Listening materials with a variety of accents

When the course is not tied to a particular country, or when we want to expose students to several different standard accents within a module on language skills, we can now offer variety. In the Expansion course (M2 public health), students hear American, British and Australian English, options that did not exist before.

4. Co-imagine

Learning objective: Use GenAI as a creative partner.
Added value: With good prompts, GenAI helps us find ideas we had not thought of for structuring a course, making it more effective, and offering varied tasks and activities we could not have designed without GenAI. It is time-consuming work: hallucinations are frequent and vigilance is essential. Sometimes the ideas are good, sometimes bad. Either way, GenAI serves as a springboard to go further, question our practices and find motivation.

Brainstorming a semester-long progression

We hold a dialogue with GenAI, using several types of prompts (one-shot, few-shot, megaprompts), to build course architectures based on our learning objectives.

Brainstorming the structure of a grammar module

We ask NotebookLM to suggest a way of organising a grammar lesson, drawing on our references and on an architecture we have already defined.

Read the prompt (PDF) PDF · 94 KB

Ideas for relevant, motivating themes, tasks or topics, activities and exercise types

GenAI can help us find relevant themes that interest students and are out of the ordinary, while being useful for everyday or professional life. The same goes for activities (for example for written or spoken production tasks) and exercise types… but not for the exercises themselves, except when a gap-fill text needs to be written.

Read the prompt (PDF) PDF · 89 KB

Help with designing prompts

We sometimes use GenAI to help us word the prompts we give students when they are very complex, to make sure we have not left out anything GenAI tools need to interpret our requests. This means setting out your objectives in detail, asking GenAI whether it has any additional information, testing the prompt and improving it with or without GenAI, and publishing the working version.

Watch a prompt being built (video) (in French)

Improving what already exists: GenAI-assisted self-assessment and critique

Once a course has been designed, GenAI becomes an outside critical eye. We ask it to take on the role of a strict expert (in language teaching, learning design ergonomics, accessibility…) and to point out, without indulgence, the weaknesses of a module: vague objectives, activities that do not serve the target skill, feedback that is too long, a shaky progression. GenAI does not replace our judgement, but it reveals blind spots and forces us to justify our choices. It is then up to us to separate what is relevant from what is not.

Learning analytics (analysing results with a view to improvement)

GenAI can be given the results of a course questionnaire which, if well designed, can be analysed by a chatbot. This lets the teacher know which points to improve for the next run of the course.

5. Draw on knowledge and sources

Learning objective: Draw on a large body of references.
Added value: It is not always possible to keep in mind all the sources we need to build our courses.

Theoretical framework from 1,000+ documents

We give NotebookLM or Claude Cowork a list of references in language teaching, creativity, training, pedagogy, game-based learning, neuroscience, the competency-based approach… We brainstorm with GenAI to create our own pedagogical approach.

Caution: This is a springboard, not a replacement. GenAI is a brainstorming partner but the risk of hallucinations is high, and it is up to the teacher-designer to make informed pedagogical decisions.

Compiling the terminology of a field

Teachers of languages for students of other disciplines (LANSAD) are not always familiar with the vocabulary of their students’ field of study. Here, GenAI can be useful for accessing expertise we do not have and drawing up lists of sentences, paragraphs or texts containing terms, with their definitions, translations and usage. From a brochure setting out the learning objectives of a degree programme, a list of themes… and terms can be drawn up.

Caution: These themes and terms will need to be validated by a subject teacher before being shared.

Sometimes it’s time wasted…

On topics such as intercultural communication, hallucinations and stereotypes make some uses almost impossible. We can only rely on a few tools such as NotebookLM and on targeted tasks that do not require sensitive data.

6. Provide models and detailed formative feedback to students

Learning objective: Design materials and feedback that help students progress.
Added value: GenAI makes it possible to offer additional support that could not be offered before for lack of resources.

Model answers

Recordings made with GenAI are offered as model answers for each spoken production topic. They are first co-written by GenAI and the teacher.

The written production model answer is produced at several levels with GenAI and reworked by the teacher, so that students can assess the level of a piece of work themselves, criterion by criterion, and fully understand what the CLES expects while seeing a model.

Answer-by-answer formative feedback

In online courses, we provide detailed feedback, with hints that allow students to correct themselves. Sometimes we run short of inspiration for varying the feedback and hints with humour while staying within the scenario: GenAI often whispers good ideas to us.

7. Create tailored resources and personalise learning

Learning objective: Calibrate each resource to a level.
Added value: GenAI makes it possible to offer students a wider variety of resources.

Recalibrating an authentic text

Authentic articles are rarely within reach of the lowest levels. GenAI can rephrase an entire text to adapt it to the desired level.

Creating a video to practise pronunciation

Audio and video generated with GenAI can be used in pronunciation exercises where students have to repeat sentences. Because teachers control the content, they can co-create texts in a specific style (e.g. an oath of allegiance) in which the terms and language points studied in the unit are reused.

On the Espace Langues website, we provide a prompt that lets each student work on vocabulary and grammar according to their own goals. The student is in the driving seat.

8. Make it accessible and multimodal

Learning objective: Support comprehension and pronunciation through images and sound.
Added value: Dual coding was not feasible before because of a lack of resources.

Images and sound to accompany narratives, concepts and examples

GenAI makes it possible to quickly generate images in a consistent style to illustrate parts of a course. It can also generate speech, with varied voices.

Automatically generated subtitles

GenAI-generated subtitles make our courses more accessible, since some students may struggle with listening comprehension, in line with the principles of Universal Design for Learning. In this example, what the DU Passerelle students said was translated so that their advice… and their accents could be better understood!

Limitations and avenues

Limitations

Engage and immerse: narrative consistency over time is fragile. GenAI can generate an effective hook scenario, but may fail to keep the characters consistent and the plot credible from one week to the next.

Contextualise: subject content must be validated by a subject expert. A fictional article on public health or any other specialist field may contain factual errors that a language teacher cannot detect. Any specialist resource generated by GenAI must be reviewed by a subject teacher.

Draw on knowledge and sources: synthesising references can betray theoretical nuances. Even when working from documents it has been given, GenAI may overlook distinctions that are crucial for teaching practice. Careful proofreading remains essential.

Supporting self-assessment: GenAI designs distractors, not questions. It can generate plausible distractors for a multiple-choice quiz if it is well prompted, but it does not assess the target skill rigorously. It cannot tell what really measures comprehension. The questions must be designed by the teacher.

Create resources: CEFR calibration is unreliable. GenAI does not have a precise command of the framework’s descriptors. A text labelled B1 may be A2 or B2 depending on the dimension. All content must be validated by the teacher.

Create tailored resources: image generation rarely works first time. Getting an image that matches exactly what you have in mind (framing, style, characters, atmosphere…) takes many iterations: rewording the prompt, adjusting details, rerunning, starting again.

Avenues we are exploring

Differentiation and accessibility: Producing several versions of the same resource to suit students’ profiles and the accommodations they need.

Adaptive learning: Adjusting content in real time according to the student’s answers. A use with strong potential, but one that requires resources we do not yet have and that remains to be developed.

Self-correction: Continuing to test template prompts for self-correction and learning.
