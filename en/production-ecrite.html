Written production

In class, feedback on writing is often delayed, partial or subjective. GenAI changes this dynamic by offering feedback that is immediate, detailed and supportive: every draft becomes an opportunity to practise, self-assess and improve. What is more, by comparing their work with the machine’s textual or visual output, students learn to analyse, to build on it and to refine their own production.

Prompt engineering and critical thinking

Precise description of a given image

Life Hacks · English · unit "AI Hacks"

The exercise

To develop students’ lexical and grammatical accuracy by having them write detailed prompts in English to reproduce an image with a GenAI tool. By analysing their results and interacting on a forum, students also strengthen their critical skills in writing.

What the student does:

  • Look closely at the image.
  • Use a chatbot to reproduce the image, focusing on the details and writing prompts that are as precise as possible.
  • Post the resulting image on the forum with a comment on the result and the difficulties encountered, and interact with the other participants’ work.

Example of an image with a comment

Their comment on the forum

"The generated image is almost similar to the original; however, it is difficult to reproduce the same cushion patterns as those in the reference image. In my image, the blue bird is also missing, as well as the moss at the base of the tree, the bushes in the background, and the rug under the cushions. The cats are also slightly different from the original; they do not have the same position. The tree is different as well, and there are additional trees and flowers that do not appear in the original model."

Exam preparation

Detailed feedback on the unit’s written production task

TrèFLE · French as a foreign language (FLE) · unit "Education"

To get feedback on a DELF-style written production (DELF: official French language certification) in order to practise before sitting the exam. The prompt uses the criteria of the official exam so that students can pinpoint exactly what they need to work on.

What the student does:

  • Write a letter to the magazine Psycho Enfants comparing different approaches to parenting, describing the methods and their consequences. Use logical connectors, the gerund (gérondif), expressions of personal opinion and the vocabulary of the unit. No human or digital help during this phase.
  • Use a chatbot to get a detailed diagnosis of their written production based on the official DELF B1 criteria. The student is given a guided prompt.
  • Read the chatbot’s feedback carefully and identify the points to work on. Share the feedback with the teacher for validation and clarification if needed.
Read the prompt
PROMPT

#I am a learner of [target language]. I would like you to act as a private [target language] tutor and explain to me the strengths and weaknesses of a [level] written production assignment. Reply only in [target language], in language suited to [level].

#Here are the success criteria for a [level] assignment (B1 criteria in our version):
## Can follow the instructions given, including all the required elements
## Can match their production to the topic set.
## Can respect the minimum length indicated
## Can describe facts, events or experiences
## Can present their ideas, feelings and/or reactions and give their opinion.
## Can use logical connectors
## Has sufficient vocabulary to talk about everyday topics, using circumlocution if necessary.
## Shows good control of basic vocabulary, but serious errors still occur when expressing more complex thoughts
## Spelling, punctuation and layout are accurate enough to be followed easily most of the time.
## Can correctly use [target grammar point]

#Here are the steps:
##I will give you the assignment topic. Do not comment on the topic.
##I will give you my answer to the assignment.
##You will comment on my assignment by giving me a list of my mistakes, your corrections of these mistakes and detailed explanations for each mistake, in simple language. Make a table with a mistakes column, a corrections column and an explanations column.
##You will give an overall comment on the strengths and weaknesses of my assignment in relation to what is expected at [level], and you will give me precise advice so that I can work on the points I did not do well.
##You will invite me to ask you questions about anything I did not understand.

#Here are the assignment instructions:
“ The magazine Psycho Enfants is preparing a special issue entitled “What makes a good parent?”. You have thought a lot about the question and you are writing a letter to the magazine comparing different approaches to parenting: you describe parents’ methods and their consequences. Organise your text well (180 words) and give your opinion with arguments and concrete examples. Use logical connectors ([examples in target language]), [target grammar point] ([example]), expressions of personal opinion ([examples in target language]) and vocabulary related to education"

Here is my assignment: [PASTE YOUR ASSIGNMENT]

Optional use of GenAI

Independent writing with optional GenAI correction

Intercultural Communication · English · unit "Melting Pots vs Mosaics"

To have students first write without any help, on a question of intercultural reflection. By freely choosing whether or not to submit their text to GenAI to analyse their mistakes, students develop their ability to self-assess and a responsible, critical relationship with the tool.

What the student does:

  • Answer the question on "melting pot" and "mosaic" societies in about 200 words, without any help (no GenAI, no machine translation).
  • Decide whether to have a chatbot analyse their production to get corrections and explanations of their mistakes, or to stop there and hand in their original version.
  • If they use GenAI, take the time to understand their mistakes and the suggested corrections, while keeping a critical eye on the suggestions.
Read the prompt
PROMPT

I am an ESL student. Please correct and explain my mistakes in a table with 2 columns (incorrect, correct) then give me detailed explanation to help me understand my mistakes, and a corrected version of my work. Then, provide general feedback using CEFR as a reference. Please give a title to each part (recap, explanations, corrected version, general feedback). Are you ready?

Professional skills

Writing a CV for an unusual job

Life Hacks · English · unit "Career Hacks"

To master the conventions of the English-language CV through humour and role play. By writing an application for an unusual job and sharing it on a forum, students acquire key terms, make the norms their own, and develop their ability to summarise as well as their digital skills in a playful way.

What the student does:

  • Read about some of the weirdest jobs in the world and choose the one they want to apply for.
  • Design the funniest CV possible, drawing on GenAI for layout and ideas if they wish.

What we observe

Limitations

Corrective feedback erases the student’s voice. When it corrects, GenAI tends to rewrite rather than correct: without a prompt explicitly saying not to rephrase the student’s sentences, the “improved” version is often an entirely reworded text, smoother but less personal.

CEFR level assessment is unreliable. GenAI assigns levels inaccurately and usually too generously. Even when trained, it does not apply the framework’s descriptors rigorously, even when given the reference documents, and may rate a text B2 where a teacher would see B1.

Assisted creation short-circuits the effort of writing. The risk is that students let GenAI produce the text for them rather than using it as a support tool: without explicit instructions, the line between scaffolding and substitution is thin.

The inability to prioritise errors. GenAI corrects everything at the same level, from a simple typo to a major misunderstanding. Without pedagogical judgement, this flood of corrections overloads the feedback and prevents students from identifying their priority areas for improvement.

Acceptance without understanding. The student does not study the feedback and does not try to understand why their wording was incorrect. They do not make progress.

GenAI does not target only what has been taught. It may flag errors on points never covered in class, or even completely miss the specific language features that the task was about.

False confidence. Broadly positive feedback from GenAI can convince students that their work is solid, whereas a teacher would spot weaknesses in it.

Too much motivation. For the image-generation activity, some students may have written their prompts in French, more motivated by the task than by practising the language.

Avenues we are exploring

The “surgical” correction prompt: Ask the chatbot to isolate only the incorrect segment and suggest a correction of one or two words at most, without touching the rest of the sentence.

The “reflective tutor” stance and Socratic AI: Ask the chatbot to highlight the error, ask a guiding question or give a grammatical hint to encourage students to self-correct, or to refuse to give the answer so that students have to find it themselves.

The prompt with examples: Include in the prompt itself real student productions calibrated by teachers (a genuine borderline B1, a genuine B2).

Honest GenAI: Include an instruction that forces the chatbot to be objective, such as: "Identify major structural or argumentative weaknesses. Do not try to reassure the student; be a neutral academic marker."

Sorting by severity: Ask the chatbot to sort its feedback into a two-level table:
1. Top priorities (meaning, grammar that blocks understanding)
2. Secondary improvements (typos, style), so that students can filter what they read
