Spoken production

To counter inhibition and the limited speaking time available in class, the voice mode of chatbots can offer an additional practice space. Students can make as many attempts as they like and use their mistakes to progress, without the immediate pressure of an exchange with a real person.

Spoken interaction

Learning objective:

To practise spontaneous spoken interaction in English, using a chatbot’s voice mode as a fictional conversation partner.

Anchored in the scenario of the teaching unit, the exchange lets students reuse the vocabulary and structures covered in class in a simulated communication situation.

What the student does:

  1. Read the scenario.
  2. Copy the prompt into a chatbot of their choice.
  3. Turn on the app’s voice mode.
  4. Play their character and interact orally with the GenAI.

To try out an activity, copy its prompt and paste it into the chatbot you use.

The AIAS label on each activity shows how much room the task leaves for GenAI, from 1 (no GenAI) to 5 (creative exploration). See the scale

Everyday dialogues

Role play after watching a clip from the TV series "Bridgerton"

Ready Set Action · English B1/B1+ · unit "Bridgerton"
Scenario

You are secretly in love with one of your friends, but you are too shy to tell them how you feel. You decide to confide in your older sister and tell her about an embarrassing experience related to this situation.

Read the prompt
PROMPT

YOUR ROLE: You will be playing the role of my older sister. Don't say you're an AI language model.

MY ROLE: I will be playing the role of someone who is in love with their friend and is too shy to tell him/her.

MY OBJECTIVE: I would like to have a simple back-and-forth conversation with you (no thinking mode).

TOPIC: The conversation will revolve around my embarrassing experience.

CONSTRAINTS: Only respond with short answers and ask me follow up questions. Please wait for my input before you reply.

Are you ready?

Job interview

Answering questions by reusing the grammar and vocabulary from the course

WordIA · English · unit "University"
Scenario

You have applied for a part-time job in a company so that you can observe humans. You are now going to have a job interview, during which you will answer questions about your past experience and what you have done on Earth since you arrived.

Read the prompt
PROMPT

Act as an employer. I am an extraterrestrial applying for a part-time job. Ask typical interview questions about my education, experience, and activities on Earth. Use a mix of past simple and present perfect to encourage both tenses. Keep your questions short, open-ended, and one at a time. Wait for my response before asking the next.

Preparing for the CLES speaking exam (debate)

Debate on the environmental impact of air travel

SocAIty · English · unit "Sustainable Skies"
Scenario

At the press conference, you debate with another journalist whether air transport can truly be sustainable. One of the journalists is highly sceptical about the industry’s environmental impact, while you believe a balance must be struck between the economic benefits of air transport and ecological concerns. In a concise ten-minute exchange, each of you will present your point of view, respond to the other’s arguments and ask open questions to clarify the underlying assumptions. Your ultimate goal is to determine whether there is a workable compromise that meets both environmental priorities and global mobility needs, or whether the differences are too great to overcome.

Read the prompt
PROMPT

I would like to have a back-and-forth conversation with you, with very short exchanges and open-ended questions. You’ll play the role of a journalist specializing in environmental issues who is skeptical about the sustainability of air travel. I’ll play the role of a journalist who believes in balancing air travel’s benefits with environmental concerns. Together, we’ll debate air travel’s environmental, economic, and ethical impacts using arguments from both sides. Challenge my points with counterarguments, and politely ask me to justify my ideas if I don’t provide enough explanation. Our goal is to reach a workable compromise before my token limit expires. Only reply with short messages and wait for my input each time. Let’s begin!

Conversation with an expert

Challenging cultural stereotypes

Intercultural Communication · English · weeks 1-2 (optional activity)
Scenario

You are talking with a PhD student who specialises in intercultural communication. After some quick research on common stereotypes about your home country, you discuss these clichés with them and how they can be challenged.

Read the prompt
PROMPT

YOUR ROLE: Please pretend you are a doctoral student working on intercultural communication. Don’t say you are a language model.

MY ROLE: I am ESL student at university learning about intercultural communication.

MY OBJECTIVE: I’d like to have a back-and-forth conversation about the following topic:

TOPIC: What are some cultural stereotypes about your country? How would you challenge those stereotypes?

CONSTRAINTS: Your replies should not exceed 25 words. Please wait for my input before you reply.

Are you ready?

Argumentative debate

Convincing a stubborn manager by reusing the grammar and vocabulary from the course

SocAIty · English · unit "Egg Freezing"
Scenario

Brace yourself, human. I, LifePlan (the AI in charge of overseeing work-life balance), am convinced that fertility benefits for young employees are unnecessary. I refuse to unlock any more human knowledge until you prove me wrong. Your mission is to debate with a fictional, stubborn manager and convince them that employer coverage of egg freezing is cost-effective, serves the public interest and respects personal well-being. Use passive constructions, hedging (politely expressed disagreement) and infinitive phrases to put your point across. Will your arguments be enough to unlock the next secrets of SocAIty?

Read the prompt
PROMPT

I want to have a back-and-forth conversation with you. Act as a very stubborn manager who insists fertility benefits are unnecessary for younger employees. LifePlan, the AI overseeing work-life balance, refuses to unlock human knowledge until I convince you that egg freezing coverage is both cost-effective and beneficial to the public. Please ask me to justify my suggestions and provide counterpoints. I must propose measures that could lead to good results, using passive constructions, polite disagreement, and infinitive phrases. Ask me one question at a time and wait for my response. Only reply with short answers. Conclude before the user’s token limit is reached.

Sustained monologue

AIAS 1No GenAIthenAIAS 3Targeted collaboration

Preparing for the CLES speaking exam (monologue)

Practising a monologue and self-correcting with GenAI

SocAIty · English · unit "Egg Freezing"

Learning objective:

This activity aims to develop sustained monologue skills under the conditions of the CLES (French university language certification).

Students record a two-minute monologue and submit the audio to a chatbot, which provides a level diagnosis and three targeted pedagogical clues (grammar, vocabulary, phonology) so that they can self-correct without being given the answers directly. They then compare their output with models to refine their register.

What the student does:

  • Prepare the talk under exam conditions: Take 5 minutes to plan, noting down only a few keywords, without writing a full script. Introduce their family situation, explain their reasons (for having children or not), describe the impact on their life, say whether they regret their decision, and give advice to listeners.
  • Record and submit: Record a 2-minute monologue, then submit the audio file to a chatbot with the prompt provided to obtain a B1 level diagnosis and three structured pedagogical clues.
  • Analyse their mistakes using the clues: Read the three clues (grammar, vocabulary, phonology), identify their mistakes without the GenAI correcting them directly, and submit their own corrections.
  • Compare with the models: Listen to the three model recordings provided and decide which one uses the register best suited to a radio programme, justifying their choice.
Read the prompt
PROMPT

You are an expert ESL Examiner. I am a student taking a B1 level speaking exam. I am submitting an audio recording of a 2-minute radio testimony about my life choices. The topic is: "10 years later, you find yourself with or without children. A radio show is seeking testimonies from individuals who have had children or not. You decide to leave a message to share your experience. Discuss the consequences of your life choices and whether you regret them or not. Introduce yourself and your family situation. Explain your reasons for wanting to have kids or not. Describe the impact of this decision on your life (positive and negative aspects). Say whether you regret this decision or not. Give advice to listeners. Speak for 2 minutes"]

Your Goal: Evaluate if my performance reaches the B1 threshold and help me self-correct to improve my skills.

B1 Level Requirements to check: Can I maintain a monologue with enough fluency to be understood? Do I use connectors (but, because, although, therefore) to link my ideas? Can I express my opinions and explain my plans/choices? Is my grammar controlled enough (even if there are mistakes, they shouldn't stop the listener from understanding)?

Your Instructions: Listen to the audio. Level Diagnosis: Tell me if my performance is currently below, at, or above the B1 level. Disclaimer: Explicitly state: 'Please note that I am an AI; this evaluation is indicative and may contain errors compared to an official human examiner.'

Guided Feedback (The 'Clue' Method): Instead of giving me the corrections, give me 3 pedagogical clues to help me improve:

Clue 1 (Grammar/Tense): Identify a sentence where the tense was incorrect for a B1 level (e.g., mixing present and past) and ask me to find it. Clue 2 (Vocabulary/Precision): Suggest a passage where my vocabulary was a bit too simple (A2 level) and ask if I can find a more 'B1' synonym or expression. Clue 3 (Phonology): Identify one keyword that was mispronounced and would hinder understanding for a native listener. Ask me to identify the correct word stress.

Structure & Strategy: Ask me one question about my choice of arguments: 'Do you think you justified your reasons clearly enough for a radio audience?'

Next Step: Tell me: 'I am waiting for your corrections on these 3 points before we proceed further.'

Tone: Professional, encouraging, and focused on B1 certification standards.

Limitations and avenues

Limitations

GenAI can be too lenient. Unless an accent is genuinely unintelligible, it is not put off by strong accents or by mistakes. It reconstructs on its own what the student was unable to express, and so removes the friction that, in a real exchange, helps learners progress.

Assisted creation short-circuits the effort of writing. The risk is that students let GenAI produce in their place rather than using it as a support tool: without explicit instructions, the line between scaffolding and substitution is thin.

Default feedback remains superficial. Left to its own devices, GenAI tends to over-mark and to give feedback that is overly encouraging, vague or uniform. Without a rubric provided by the teacher, it does not assess: it reassures.

Use is neither required nor systematically documented, so it is hard to measure. Turning to GenAI is a voluntary, spontaneous choice. Teachers do not know who used it, except through self-reported use in questionnaires.

Phonological feedback is not reliable. Students cannot always count on GenAI to explain pronunciation or give the correct phonetic transcription.

Technical limitations. The conversation may cut out, especially when there is background noise.

Avenues we are exploring

Practising to reduce anxiety: GenAI can act as a practice partner before a marked speaking task.

Making the most of adaptivity: GenAI can adjust the complexity of its responses to the student’s level.

Making GenAI less tolerant: set up the GenAI so that it pushes students to clarify what they mean when necessary.

Introducing a conditional trigger: ask the GenAI to unlock the next part of the exchange only once the student has produced a specific linguistic form.
